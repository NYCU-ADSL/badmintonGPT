# Creating multimodal badminton match highlights with narrative structure

### **II. Research methods**

The core goal of this research is to automatically transform structured shot-by-shot badminton match data into a highlight video with a complete dramatic narrative, ready for Instagram Reels or YouTube Shorts. We designed an end-to-end architecture spanning data parsing → highlight selection → narrative arrangement → multi-agent script generation → multimodal synthesis → system integration. The three core innovations are **(1) highlight arrangement centered on a narrative arc**, **(2) script generation through a Writer–Critic–Audience multi-agent quality loop**, and **(3) automatic synthesis coordinating speech, a virtual presenter, and video**. Each is described below.

#### **2.1 System overview and end-to-end architecture**

Traditional match highlights require professional editors and narration writers, making production time-consuming and hard to scale. Existing automated methods often stop at selecting and concatenating exciting clips, lacking a coherent storyline and spoken narration; the result resembles a montage rather than a short video viewers watch through. This subproject therefore proposes a complete generation workflow that both selects highlights and **tells the match as a story with a beginning, development, turn, and conclusion, like a news presenter**.

The workflow first loads shot annotations and pre-cut rally videos. A match-analysis module then uses **rule-based excitement scoring** to select 5–8 highlight rallies and assign narrative roles. An LLM-driven multi-agent loop generates and repeatedly revises a Chinese narration script. Speech synthesis, virtual presenter generation, and video composition then produce the final short video.

#### **2.2 Structured match data parsing and highlight selection**

This subproject uses ShuttleSet shot annotations. Each match includes a rally segmentation table (RallySeg), per-game shot labels, and pre-cut rally videos. Annotations provide more than 30 fields per shot, including shot type, hitting and landing areas, and reasons for winning/losing points, establishing the factual basis for scoring and narration.

**Rule-based excitement scoring.** We designed an interpretable heuristic scoring function that measures rally excitement along four dimensions and combines them by weight:

* **Rally length (30%)**: shot count reflects the excitement of exchanges, calculated as `min(shot_count ÷ 25 × 100, 100)`.
* **Winning method (20%)**: an active winner landing in court scores 80; passive points from opponent errors such as hitting into the net, out of bounds, or failing to clear the net score 40; other outcomes score 0.
* **Technical difficulty (20%)**: count difficult shots (smashes, net kills, stick smashes), around-the-head shots, and backhand attacks. After scoring each event, calculate `min(technical_count × 15, 100)`.
* **Score context (30%)**: score the tension of the current scoreline. A close endgame (highest score ≥20 and difference ≤1) receives 100; tight exchanges from 19 points receive 80; midgame contests receive progressively lower scores; early play or large margins receive the minimum 20.

**Selection strategy.** Select at most N rallies in descending excitement order, with **game-diversity guarantees**: if a game has no selected rallies, add its highest-scoring rally so highlights do not overconcentrate on one game and lose overall representativeness.

The four weights are currently manual. Once enough human highlight/non-highlight labels are collected, learnable weights such as learning-to-rank could replace them.

#### **2.3 Narrative arrangement: a five-act arc and ten-part news structure**

Match excitement is uneven. Simply concatenating high-scoring rallies often lacks narrative development and fails to sustain attention. We therefore designed a **rule-based** arrangement mapping selected rallies onto a five-act arc within a fixed news-script skeleton, combining information density and dramatic tension.

**Five-act narrative arc.** The system takes **the most exciting selected rally as the hook and moves it to the beginning** (a flashback structure) to capture attention in the first three seconds. Remaining rallies retain their **original chronological order** and are proportionally divided into four stages: first 30% buildup, 30%–70% turning point, 70%–90% climax, and the remainder ending.

**Ten-part news structure.** On top of this arc, the Writer agent (2.4) organizes a fixed ten-part news script: ① hook → ② presenter opening → ③ match background → ④–⑧ five key rally analyses → ⑨ match summary → ⑩ presenter closing.

Currently, fixed proportional thresholds divide the acts. A future data-driven approach could adapt boundaries and lengths to score dynamics and critical-point distributions, aligning pacing with the match's actual tension curve.

#### **2.4 Multi-agent script generation and quality loop**

Narration quality directly affects watchability, but a single LLM call often struggles to balance pacing, factual accuracy, and entertainment. We propose a **Generate–Evaluate–ReGenerate (G-E-RG) multi-agent loop** based on LangGraph StateGraph, improving scripts through collaboration and repeated revision by Writer, Critic, and Audience agents.

**Overall flow.** Nodes run Writer → Critic → Audience → Quality Gate, followed by a conditional branch that either ends or returns to Writer for revision.

**Writer agent.** Generates and revises Chinese narration scripts with embedded Fish Speech emotion tags such as `[excited]` and `[whispering]`. These **bridge text and speech prosody**, directly driving vocal emotion during synthesis.

**Critic agent.** Simulates a critic, scoring five dimensions from 0–10 and combining them by weight: pacing (25%), accuracy (20%), emotional arc (20%), entertainment (20%), and duration (15%), to meet system design and user needs.

**Audience agent.** Uses four **LLM-simulated personas**—casual viewer, badminton enthusiast, heavy short-video user, and sports presenter—to predict watch-through, engagement (0–10), and willingness to share. These are aggregated into mean watch-through, mean engagement, and share rate.

**Quality Gate.** Fixed thresholds require **all three conditions simultaneously**: critic score ≥7.0, mean watch-through ≥70%, and mean engagement ≥6.0. Otherwise, the previous script and specific Critic/Audience feedback return to Writer for revision.

The current Critic and Audience agents are prompt-engineered LLM judges. Future real-viewer completion and engagement data could train a lightweight reward model to calibrate or replace simulated judges, bringing the quality loop closer to actual audience reactions.

#### **2.5 Multimodal synthesis: emotional speech, virtual presenter, and video**

After script completion, multimodal synthesis transforms textual narration into a complete video with **speech, virtual presenter, and subtitles**.

**Emotional speech synthesis.** Fish Speech's cloud service synthesizes narration segment by segment, passing embedded emotion tags unchanged to produce corresponding vocal dynamics.

**Virtual presenter generation.** Speech and a presenter reference photo are sent to the EchoMimicV3 talking-head service to generate lip-synchronized presenter video (128p).

**Subtitles and video composition.** The composition module combines all modalities. It first generates subtitles, then maps each segment's rally_id (score string, e.g. 1_05_04) to the corresponding rally video.

The virtual presenter currently uses a fixed reference image and generic voice. Future custom voice cloning and specified presenter portraits could let users choose their preferred presenter.
